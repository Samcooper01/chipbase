What's in the harness:

systolic-array-ai-accelerator-harness/
  design/          # the fixed inputs: RTL (Register-Transfer Level),
                   # SRAM macros, activity file
  flow/            # the reference flow (flow/gemmini/) and the evaluator
  reproduce/       # the reference packaged as an entry
  judge/           # the official runner (step 4)
  setup.sh         # step 1
  run_baseline.sh  # steps 2 and 4: runs the flow
  evaluate.sh      # step 4: scores a result

# made as you go, never committed:
  work/            # the flow's working files (about 40 GB per run)
  baseline/        # the flow's result
  judge_work/      # your entry, built as the official run builds it
  eval_work/       # the evaluator's work and evaluation.json
{% endblock %} {% block brief_items %}
  • Open-source tools, or your own: write your own placer, router, or scripts if you like, as long as it is open source and in your repository.
  • Physical design changes only: floorplan, placement, clock tree, routing, sizing, and buffering are yours. Synthesis is pinned, and the evaluator proves your final netlist against its own run of it.
  • Only the SRAM macros are fixed: everything else you can harden, split, or leave flat, and you place all of it.
  • Your repository must build the layout from the RTL within the 12 hour budget, on 16 threads and 64 GB of memory.
  • The evaluator takes every measurement itself from your layouts, so every entry is scored the same way.

    Understand the design

    • One matrix multiply, step by step: New to systolic arrays? Start here.
    • Gemmini's documentation: The architecture, straight from the designers at the University of California, Berkeley.
    • The Gemmini paper (Genc et al., Design Automation Conference 2021): Why the blocks are shaped the way they are.

    Learn the steps you will change

    • Floorplanning: Where the macros go, and why it matters most here.
    • Placement: Spreading the standard cells around the macros.
    • Clock tree synthesis: One clock to every tile and memory bank.
    • Routing: Connecting it all on nine metal layers.
    • Static timing analysis: How the evaluator decides your clock period is real.
    • Methods and algorithms: The algorithms behind each stage, if you want to write your own tools.

    Know the process

    • ASAP7, the predictive 7 nm process kit: The process the challenge is built on.
    • The metal stack and design rules: The layers you route on and the rules the design-rule check enforces.
    • PVT (Process, Voltage, Temperature) corners: Why setup is checked at the slow corner and hold at the fast.

    Practice first

    • Harden riscv32i in your browser: Take a real processor through the flow in seconds, and see what the clock and utilization do.
    • Graded problems: Small physical design tasks, some graded by a real OpenROAD run.

    Tool documentation

    • OpenROAD: The place-and-route tool the reference flow uses.
    • OpenROAD-flow-scripts: The flow the harness pins, and its settings.
