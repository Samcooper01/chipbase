  • Open-source tools, or your own: write your own placer, router, or scripts if you like, as long as it is open source and in your repository.
  • Physical design changes only: floorplan, placement, clock tree, routing, sizing, and buffering are yours. Synthesis is pinned, and the evaluator proves your final netlist against its own run of it.
  • The core is built flat, as one layout, and has no memory macros.
  • Your repository must build the layout from the RTL within the 1 hour budget, on 8 threads and 16 GB of memory.
  • The evaluator takes every measurement itself from your layout, so every entry is scored the same way.

    Understand the design

    • Ibex's documentation: How the core works, from lowRISC, who maintain it.
    • Ibex on GitHub: The source, and the configurations beyond the one pinned here.

    Learn the steps you will change

    • Floorplanning: Die size, shape and pin placement, which set how far signals travel.
    • Placement: Where the cells go, which sets the length of the critical path.
    • Clock tree synthesis: One clock to every flip-flop, with useful skew if you want it.
    • Routing: Connecting it all on nine metal layers.
    • Static timing analysis: How the evaluator decides your clock period is real.
    • Methods and algorithms: The algorithms behind each stage, if you want to write your own tools.

    Know the process

    • ASAP7, the predictive 7 nm process kit: The process the warmup is built on.
    • The metal stack and design rules: The layers you route on and the rules the design-rule check enforces.
    • PVT (Process, Voltage, Temperature) corners: Why setup is checked at the slow corner and hold at the fast.

    Practice first

    • Harden riscv32i in your browser: Take a simpler processor through the flow in seconds, and see what the clock and utilization do.
    • Graded problems: Small physical design tasks, some graded by a real OpenROAD run.

    Ready for more

    • Chipbase Competition 01: A 16 by 16 systolic-array AI accelerator, with memory macros, partitioning and a 12 hour budget.

    Tool documentation

    • OpenROAD: The place-and-route tool the reference flow uses.
    • OpenROAD-flow-scripts: The flow the harness pins, and its settings.
